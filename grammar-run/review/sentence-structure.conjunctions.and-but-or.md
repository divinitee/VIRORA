## transform (15)

1. _Join the two sentences into one with and. Use the word in brackets._  
   Aziz plays **volleyball** **at weekends**. Bobur plays **volleyball** **at weekends** too.  
   → Aziz ______ (Bobur) play **volleyball** **at weekends**.  
   key: and Bobur  
   why: Two people do the same thing, so join them with and: Aziz and Bobur play…

2. _Join the two sentences into one with and. Use the word in brackets._  
   I bought **apples** at the **bazaar**. I bought bread too.  
   → I bought **apples** ______ (bread) at the **bazaar**.  
   key: and bread  
   why: Add one more thing with and: … and bread.

3. _Join the two sentences into one with and. Use the word in brackets._  
   We visited **Tashkent** **last summer**. We visited Khiva too.  
   → We visited **Tashkent** ______ (Khiva) **last summer**.  
   key: and Khiva  
   why: Add one more place with and: … and Khiva.

4. _Join the two sentences into one with and. Use the word in brackets._  
   Lola can sing at **parties** **very well**. She can dance there too.  
   → Lola can sing ______ (dance) at **parties** **very well**.  
   key: and dance  
   why: Add one more action with and: sing and dance.

5. _Join the two sentences into one with and. Use the word in brackets._  
   My **bedroom** has **a bed**. It has a lamp too.  
   → My **bedroom** has **a bed** ______ (lamp).  
   key: and a lamp  
   why: Add one more thing with and: … and a lamp.

6. _Join the two sentences into one with but. Use the word in brackets._  
   I like **plov**. I don't like fish.  
   → I like **plov**, ______ (fish).  
   key: but not fish (also: but i don't like fish, but i do not like fish)  
   why: The second part is a contrast, so use but: … but not fish.

7. _Join the two sentences into one with but. Use the word in brackets._  
   Olga speaks **Korean** **a little**. She doesn't speak Chinese.  
   → Olga speaks **Korean** **a little**, ______ (Chinese).  
   key: but not Chinese (also: but she doesn't speak chinese, but she does not speak chinese)  
   why: A contrast: use but: … but not Chinese.

8. _Join the two sentences into one with but. Use the word in brackets._  
   We have **pears** **today**. We don't have bananas.  
   → We have **pears** **today**, ______ (bananas).  
   key: but no bananas (also: but not bananas, but we don't have bananas, but we do not have bananas)  
   why: A contrast: use but: … but no bananas.

9. _Join the two sentences into one with but. Use the word in brackets._  
   My brother can **ride a horse**. He can't swim.  
   → My brother can **ride a horse**, ______ (swim).  
   key: but he can't swim (also: but not swim)  
   why: A contrast: use but: … but he can't swim.

10. _Join the two sentences into one with but. Use the word in brackets._  
   Aziz wanted to go to the **concert** **last night**. He was ill.  
   → Aziz wanted to go to the **concert** **last night**, ______ (ill).  
   key: but he was ill  
   why: What happened is different from what he wanted, so use but: … but he was ill.

11. _Join the two questions or sentences into one with or. Use the word in brackets._  
   Do you want **tea**? Do you want juice?  
   → Do you want **tea** ______ (juice), **Lola**?  
   key: or juice  
   why: A choice between two things: use or: … or juice?

12. _Join the two questions or sentences into one with or. Use the word in brackets._  
   Is the **party** on **Thursday**? Is it on Friday?  
   → Is the **party** on **Thursday** ______ (Friday)?  
   key: or Friday (also: or on friday)  
   why: Only one day is right, so use or: … or Friday?

13. _Join the two questions or sentences into one with or. Use the word in brackets._  
   Are you coming by **metro**? Are you walking?  
   → Are you coming by **metro** ______ (walk), **Kamila**?  
   key: or walking (also: or are you walking)  
   why: A choice between two ways: use or: … or walking?

14. _Join the two questions or sentences into one with or. Use the word in brackets._  
   Shall we meet at **six**? Shall we meet at eight?  
   → Shall we meet at **six** ______ (eight), **Anvar**?  
   key: or eight (also: or at eight)  
   why: A choice between two times: use or: … or eight?

15. _Join the two questions or sentences into one with or. Use the word in brackets._  
   Hurry up! If you don't, you'll miss the **plane**.  
   → Hurry up, ______ (miss) the **plane**!  
   key: or you'll miss  
   why: Or after an order means 'if you don't': Hurry up, or you'll miss…

## express (5)

1. Describe yourself: what you like, what you don't like, and what you can do. Write three or four sentences.  
   target: and, but for addition and contrast · look for: and to add · but for a contrast · full sentences

2. You are planning a party. Write a message to a friend with two choices for food and drinks.  
   target: or for choices · look for: or in a question · and to list things · two choices

3. Describe your city or village: what's good and what's not so good. Write three or four sentences.  
   target: and / but · look for: and to add good things · but for a problem · place names

4. Write about your last holiday: what you did and one thing that went wrong.  
   target: and / but in the past · look for: and between two actions · but for the problem · past verbs

5. Compare two people in your family. Write three or four sentences with and, but and or.  
   target: and, but, or · look for: and · but · or in a question or a choice

## transform (15)

1. _Make a question._  
   She has got a **red** bike, **Olga**.  
   → ______ a **red** bike, **Olga**?  
   key: Has she got  
   why: Put has before she, and got after she: Has she got …?

2. _Make a question._  
   They have got a **dog** called **Max**.  
   → ______ a **dog** called **Max**?  
   key: Have they got  
   why: Put have before they, and got after they: Have they got …?

3. _Make a question._  
   Your brother has got a **camera**, **Timur**.  
   → ______ a **camera**, **Timur**?  
   key: Has your brother got  
   why: Has comes first, then the person, then got: Has your brother got …?

4. _Make a question._  
   You have got a **ruler** in your bag, **Lola**.  
   → ______ a **ruler** in your bag, **Lola**?  
   key: Have you got  
   why: Have comes first, then you, then got: Have you got …?

5. _Make a question._  
   The flat has got a **balcony**, **Olga**.  
   → ______ a **balcony**, **Olga**?  
   key: Has the flat got  
   why: Has comes first, then the flat, then got: Has the flat got …?

6. _Correct the mistake._  
   Does she got a **parrot**, **Olga**?  
   → ______ she got a **parrot**, **Olga**?  
   key: Has  
   why: With got, the question starts with Has, not Does.

7. _Correct the mistake._  
   Have he got a **bike**, **Lola**?  
   → ______ he got a **bike**, **Lola**?  
   key: Has  
   why: He → Has he got …?, not Have.

8. _Correct the mistake._  
   Has they got a **red** car, **Lola**?  
   → ______ they got a **red** car, **Lola**?  
   key: Have  
   why: They → Have they got …?, not Has.

9. _Correct the mistake._  
   Is your sister got a **car**, **Olga**?  
   → ______ your sister got a **car**, **Olga**?  
   key: Has  
   why: The question with got starts with Has, not Is.

10. _Correct the mistake._  
   Have got you a **pen**, **Lola**?  
   → ______ a **pen**, **Lola**?  
   key: Have you got  
   why: The order is Have + you + got: Have you got …?

## create (8)

1. Ask a new classmate one question with Have you got … ?  
   must use: Have you got … ? · exactly one question · start with Have you got · a thing word

2. Ask a friend one question about his or her brother or sister.  
   must use: Has he / she got … ? · exactly one question · start with Has · got

3. Ask one question about a friend's flat or house.  
   must use: Has your flat / house got … ? · exactly one question · start with Has · a room word

4. You are in a shop. Ask the shop assistant one question.  
   must use: Have you got any … ? · exactly one question · Have you got any · a food or thing word

5. Ask a friend one question about pets, then write his or her short answer.  
   must use: Have you got … ? – Yes, I have. / No, I haven't. · one question and one short answer · Have you got · Yes, I have or No, I haven't

6. Ask one question about your town or village with Has … got … ?  
   must use: Has our town got … ? · exactly one question · start with Has · a place word

7. Ask your teacher one question about the lesson.  
   must use: Have we got … ? · exactly one question · start with Have we got · a school word

8. Write two questions for a new friend: one with Have you got and one with Has your … got.  
   must use: Have you got … ? / Has your … got … ? · exactly two questions · Have you got once · Has your once

## express (5)

1. You want to know a new classmate. Write three or four questions with Have you got … ? and answer one of them.  
   target: Have you got … ? · look for: Have you got · a short answer · three or four questions

2. Your friend is moving to a new flat. Ask three or four questions about it.  
   target: Has it got … ? · look for: Has it got / Has the flat got · a room word · three or four questions

3. You are going on a picnic with friends. Ask three or four questions to check what you have got.  
   target: Have we got … ? / Has … got … ? · look for: Have we got · Has … got once · three or four questions

4. Ask a friend about his or her family: brothers, sisters, cousins and pets.  
   target: Have you got … ? / Has he / she got … ? · look for: Have you got · Has he or she got · three or four questions

5. Write a short dialogue in a shop: you ask about three things, and the assistant answers.  
   target: Have you got any … ? – Yes, we have. / No, we haven't. · look for: Have you got any · a short answer · three or four lines
